About

I am a quantitative researcher and data scientist with over three years of research and data-analysis experience across three institutions. I hold an MSc in Behavioural Science from the London School of Economics (GPA 69%, High Merit) and am completing an MSc in Economics at the University of Cologne (GPA 1.0, Distinction, top 1%) — graduating 2026. I am currently on exchange at the University of Tokyo studying Data Science for Public Policy.

Since 2022, I have worked in Python and R on data ingestion, statistical modelling, machine learning, and reproducible analysis pipelines — at the Max-Planck-Institute for the Study of Societies (large cross-country panel datasets in R), the University of Cologne (Python-based experimental research, nationwide survey data, Git version control), and the London School of Economics (policy analysis and quantitative methods). My work consistently involves presenting quantitative findings clearly for both specialist and non-specialist audiences.

My statistical training covers machine learning, causal inference, Bayesian modelling, and econometric theory — supported by teaching experience in Inferential Statistics and Theoretical Econometrics. My MSc in Behavioural Science from LSE provides additional grounding in the cognitive and decision-theoretic processes underlying behavioural data, enabling more careful interpretation of findings involving human actors.

Research in Progress

A Test for the Practical Relevance of Endogeneity in Semiparametric Distribution Regression

MSc Thesis | University of Cologne — Empirical Methods & Data Analysis | 2026 | Supervised by Prof. Dr. Dominik Wied

Develops a novel asymptotically pivotal test for whether endogeneity materially affects semiparametric distribution regression estimates. Defines an L²-distance functional between the naive and IV/control-function DR estimators, derives joint influence-function representations, and constructs a self-normalized test statistic whose limiting distribution is a ratio of Brownian-motion functionals — no bootstrap required. Validated through extensive Monte Carlo simulations in R. Applied to Card (1995) returns-to-education data: IV correction is practically relevant only for high-education workers, a heterogeneous finding invisible to the scalar Wu–Hausman test.

The Effect of Cash Transfers on Political Support

Policy Analysis Essay | LSE — Quantitative Methods | 2024

Extension of Manacorda et al. (2011) using Regression Discontinuity Design in Stata. Full analysis pipeline: McCrary density tests, placebo regressions, donut-hole robustness checks, bandwidth sensitivity analysis, and local polynomial models. Finds a robust ~10% causal increase in political support from Uruguay's PANES cash transfer programme.

Distribution Sensitive Policy Analysis based on WELLBYs

MSc Dissertation | LSE — Behavioural Science | 2024 | Supervised by Dr. Christian Krekel

First study to construct a Prioritarian Social Welfare Function using life satisfaction. Applied Difference-in-Differences with Propensity Score Matching on Australia's HILDA panel survey (n > 17,000) to estimate the distributional wellbeing impact of Victoria's COVID-19 lockdown — revealing disproportionate effects on the worst-off.
