About Me

I am a quantitative researcher specialising in data science for public policy, combining rigorous empirical methods with a strong foundation in economics and behavioural science. I hold an MSc in Behavioural Science from the London School of Economics (GPA 69%, High Merit) and am currently completing an MSc in Economics at the University of Cologne (GPA 1.0, Distinction, top 1%), with an upcoming exchange at the University of Tokyo studying Data Science for Public Policy.

My work sits at the intersection of causal inference and policy analysis. At the Max-Planck-Institute for the Study of Societies, I apply synthetic control methods in R to analyze the causal impact of European institutions on macroeconomic outcomes and build reproducible panel datasets. At LSE, I used Regression Discontinuity Design and Difference-in-Differences in Stata to evaluate how cash transfers affect political support and how COVID-19 lockdowns affected well-being across income groups. I also design and program controlled behavioral experiments in Python at the Cologne Laboratory for Economic Research.

I am driven by the question of how data-driven evidence can improve policy for those who need it most — whether through distributional analysis, causal evaluation of interventions, or new welfare accounting frameworks.

What I Do

Causal Inference & Policy Evaluation

Difference-in-Differences, Regression Discontinuity Design, Synthetic Control — applied to real-world interventions in health, welfare, and political economy.

Data Analysis & Visualisation

Advanced R and Stata for panel data, spatial analysis, and reproducible research pipelines. Python for experimental data and behavioral studies.

Distributional & Welfare Analysis

Featured Work

The Effect of Cash Transfers on Political Support

Policy Analysis Essay | LSE — Quantitative Methods | 2024 | Grade: 83%

Replication and extension of Manacorda et al. (2011) using Regression Discontinuity Design in Stata. Full analysis pipeline: McCrary density tests, placebo regressions, donut-hole robustness checks, and local polynomial models. Finds a robust ~10% increase in political support from PANES cash transfers in Uruguay.

RDD Stata Policy Evaluation

Distribution Sensitive Policy Analysis based on WELLBYs

MSc Dissertation | LSE — Behavioural Science | 2024 | Supervised by Dr. Christian Krekel | Grade: 65%

First study to construct a Prioritarian Social Welfare Function using life satisfaction. Applied Difference-in-Differences on Australia's HILDA panel survey with Propensity Score Matching to estimate the distributional wellbeing impact of Victoria's COVID-19 lockdown — revealing disproportionate effects on the worst-off.
